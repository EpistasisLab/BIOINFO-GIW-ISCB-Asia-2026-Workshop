Heart failure after MI: literature and data report

  • Question: What are the main risk factors for heart failure after acute MI, and which of them could we use to predict it in our patients?
  • Date: 2026-10-22

Part 1. What the literature says (Literature Scout)

Summary of Findings:

1. Multiplex Apolipoprotein Panel and Cardiovascular Prediction (PMID: 40995631)

  • Design & Population: This study analyzed serum samples from 11,843 participants in the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial, focusing on patients post-acute coronary syndrome treated with statins.
  • Main Findings: A 9-plex apolipoprotein panel showed improved prediction of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and all-cause death compared to traditional lipid panels (AUC 0.648 vs. 0.579). The panel also helped identify patients who benefit from PCSK9 inhibitor therapy.
  • Implications: Biomarkers like apolipoproteins could serve as effective predictors for post-MI heart failure by identifying high-risk patients.

2. Beta-Blockers Post-MI and Sex-Based Differences (PMID: 40884211)

  • Design & Population: In the REBOOT trial, 8,438 patients with post-MI left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) >40% were analyzed, with a focus on sex differences.
  • Main Findings: Beta-blocker therapy seemed to increase mortality in women (especially those with preserved LVEF and higher doses) but not in men. Women also had higher rates of post-MI adverse outcomes.
  • Implications: Sex and individual cardiac function should be carefully considered when prescribing beta-blockers to prevent adverse cardiovascular outcomes.

3. RSV Hospitalization and Post-MI Cardiovascular Risks (PMID: 40503659)

  • Design & Population: A retrospective study of 471 hospitalized patients with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) analyzed cardiovascular events (CVEs) over a high-risk (28-day) and late-risk (6-month post-discharge) period.
  • Main Findings: Older adults with preexisting conditions like hypertension or coronary artery disease were particularly prone to heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and other cardiovascular complications following RSV hospitalization.
  • Implications: Identifiable comorbid conditions can help predict heart failure risk after MI, especially when compounded by infections like RSV.

4. Rheumatoid Arthritis and Cardiovascular Risks (PMID: 41184868)

  • Design & Population: Using data from the UK Biobank, this study included 1,436 rheumatoid arthritis (RA) patients and 476,975 controls to examine cardiovascular risks over 14 years.
  • Main Findings: RA patients had significantly higher risks of acute myocardial infarction, heart failure, pericardial disease, and other cardiovascular conditions, with Mendelian analysis suggesting causal links between RA and heart failure.
  • Implications: Chronic inflammatory conditions like RA should be considered risk factors for post-MI heart failure, with genetic predisposition playing a possible role as well.

5. Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-cell Therapy (CAR-T) and Cardiovascular Outcomes (PMID: 42199067)

  • Design & Population: A study of 3,292 older adults (age >65) undergoing CAR-T therapy identified cardiovascular risk factors and outcomes.
  • Main Findings: Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events occurred in 5.8% of patients, with atrial fibrillation, cardiomyopathy, and pre-treatment cerebrovascular disease being significant predictors of risk. These events increased in-hospital and 1-year mortality.
  • Implications: Specific cardiac comorbidities could be used to predict which patients may experience adverse cardiovascular outcomes after MI or therapeutic interventions.

What This Means for Our Team:

These studies suggest that comprehensive risk profiling (including biomarkers, comorbid conditions, and genetic predispositions) and personalized interventions could significantly enhance the prediction of heart failure risk post-MI. Incorporating advanced tools like apolipoprotein panels, sex-specific therapeutic adjustments, and consideration of comorbid infections or chronic diseases into clinical models should improve patient outcomes.

2. Candidate Feature Sets

Set 1: Admission Variables

  • Variables: age, sex, hypertension_stage, prior_heart_failure, atrial_fibrillation_at_admission, recent_coronary_disease, symptomatic_hypertension.
  • Rationale: All variables are known at admission, enabling early identification of high-risk patients. The features are selected based on their clinical relevance and valid associations with heart failure or related risk factors.

Set 2: Admission + Day 3 Variables

  • Variables: All variables from Set 1 plus opioids_icu_day_3, nsaids_icu_day_3, pain_relapse_day_3.
  • Rationale: Adds key information from the first three hospital days that could indicate evolving complications or responses to treatment, improving prediction accuracy at a slight delay.

Set 3: Comprehensive Predictive Model

  • Variables: All from Sets 1 and 2, plus systolic_bp_icu, diabetes, chronic_bronchitis, anterior_mi, lateral_mi.
  • Rationale: Incorporates comorbidities and myocardial infarction details that are moderately associated with HF, providing a broad view of patient risk factors.

3. Data Issues

  1. Variables to Leave Out:
  • family_history_coronary_disease: Missing in 95.76%; lacks sufficient records for analysis.
  • systolic_bp_emergency_team, diastolic_bp_emergency_team: Missing in 63.29%; unreliable for modeling.
  • serum_cpk: 99.76% missing data.
  1. Small Group Concerns:
  • Variables like history_ventricular_fibrillation (n=1), history_incomplete_lbbb (n=1), etc., have very few cases and may bias models.
  1. Proximity to Outcome:
  • Avoid using variables like opioids_icu_day_3, nsaids_icu_day_3, and pain_relapse_day_3 in early-prediction models, as they are influenced by ongoing HF treatment rather than predictive of it.

4. Predictive Questions We Can Answer

  1. Question: Can we predict heart failure at admission based on baseline characteristics?
  • Outcome: heart_failure.
  • Predictors: age, sex, hypertension_stage, prior_heart_failure, atrial_fibrillation_at_admission, recent_coronary_disease.
  • Method: Logistic regression or decision tree.
  1. Question: Does hypertension severity contribute to heart failure risk beyond age and sex?
  • Outcome: heart_failure.
  • Predictors: hypertension_stage, symptomatic_hypertension, age, sex.
  • Method: Logistic regression with interaction terms for hypertension_stage × age and hypertension_stage × sex.
  1. Question: How predictive are atrial fibrillation and prior heart failure of in-hospital heart failure events?
  • Outcome: heart_failure.
  • Predictors: prior_heart_failure, atrial_fibrillation_at_admission, ecg_rhythm_atrial_fibrillation, history_arrhythmia.
  • Method: Random forest for feature importance ranking.
  1. Question: Does the patient's history of myocardial infarction (anterior/lateral) enhance risk prediction?
  • Outcome: heart_failure.
  • Predictors: anterior_mi, lateral_mi, age, sex.
  • Method: Logistic regression or support vector machine.
  1. Question: Does the 3-day clinical course improve predictive performance compared to baseline admission data alone?
  • Outcome: heart_failure.
  • Predictors: age, sex, hypertension_stage, opioids_icu_day_3, nsaids_icu_day_3, pain_relapse_day_3.
  • Method: Compare performance using logistic regression with Set 1 and Set 2 variables.

5. Questions We Cannot Answer

  1. Can biomarker panels (e.g., apolipoproteins) improve prediction of heart failure after MI?
  • Reason: No biomarker data in our dataset.
  1. What is the impact of RSV or RA on risk of heart failure post-MI?
  • Reason: No data on infections like RSV or inflammatory conditions like RA.
  1. Does left or right ventricular systolic dysfunction predict heart failure better in our population?
  • Reason: No echocardiographic or similar imaging data available.
