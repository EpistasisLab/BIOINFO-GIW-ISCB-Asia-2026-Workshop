Heart failure after MI: literature and data report

  • Question: What are the main risk factors for heart failure after acute MI, and which of them could we use to predict it in our patients?
  • Date: 2026-11-11

Part 1. What the literature says (Literature Scout)

What the evidence shows

Heart failure after myocardial infarction: incidence and predictors

  • Narrative review summarizing the contemporary epidemiology of heart failure (HF) after myocardial infarction (MI) and the risk factors/bio‑, genetic- and imaging biomarkers that predict it (PMID 33319509).
  • Concludes that HF is still a frequent complication of MI, and that a multimodality approach to identify at-risk patients plus early guideline-directed therapy can lower the HF burden (PMID 33319509).

Incident heart failure and recurrent coronary events following acute myocardial infarction

  • Multicenter registry study of 6,804 patients discharged after acute MI from 28 Baylor Scott & White hospitals (2015–2021), 76.7% NSTEMI, median age 69 years (PMID 39874177).
  • Among patients without prior HF, 23.8% developed incident HF within 3 months of discharge, and the risk of incident HF exceeded that of recurrent MI — even among patients with EF >50% and no in-hospital HF, where 11.8% developed HF vs 3.5% recurrent MI within 1 year; incident HF was linked to higher death and rehospitalization regardless of ejection fraction (PMID 39874177).

Heart Failure Incidence Following ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction

  • Retrospective analysis of 700 consecutive STEMI patients treated with primary PCI at a tertiary center, median follow-up 43.6 months (PMID 34819233).
  • HF occurred in 15.7% and independently tripled the risk of dying (HR 3.52); independent predictors of HF were older age (HR 1.03/year), diabetes (HR 1.85), longer door-to-balloon time, Killip-Kimball class ≥II (HR 2.24) and LVEF <50% (HR 1.71), with LVEF <50% present in 76% of those who developed HF (PMID 34819233).

Comparing and contrasting risk factors for heart failure in patients with and without history of myocardial infarction (HOMAGE and UK Biobank)

  • Cohort study of 26,478 HOMAGE participants (7,241 with prior MI) validated in 500,001 UK Biobank participants (4,555 with prior MI) (PMID 35365899).
  • Older age, male sex and higher heart rate predicted HF onset both with and without prior MI, and lower renal function was more strongly associated with HF in those with prior MI; by contrast, higher BMI, systolic blood pressure, blood glucose, diabetes and hypertension predicted HF mainly in patients without prior MI (PMID 35365899).

Risk prediction models for heart failure after myocardial infarction: A systematic review

  • Systematic review of 28 studies (25 retrospective, 3 prospective; all Chinese AMI cohorts) building post-MI HF prediction models (PMID 40725875).
  • Models performed moderately (C-statistic/AUC 0.6–0.9), most used logistic regression, and the commonest predictors were age, Gensini score, ultrasensitive C-reactive protein, multivessel disease, LVEF and GRACE score; only 2 studies performed external validation and risk of bias was high in 26 of 28 models (PMID 40725875).

Risk prediction model construction for post myocardial infarction heart failure by blood immune B cells

  • Transcriptomic study (single-cell and bulk RNA sequencing of peripheral blood from MI patients with vs without subsequent HF) with independent PCR-based validation (PMID 37287974).
  • An immune-activated B-cell subtype distinguished post-MI HF from non-HF patients, and a 13-marker gene model predicted post-MI HF risk, with STING1, HSPB1, CCL5, ACTN1 and ITGB2 consistently upregulated (PMID 37287974).

S100A8/A9 as a prognostic biomarker with causal effects for post-acute myocardial infarction heart failure

  • Observational study in 1,062 (discovery) and 1,043 (validation) acute MI patients with in-hospital and long-term HF outcomes, plus Mendelian randomization in three independent cohorts (PMID 38538601).
  • Elevated circulating S100A8/A9 (a pro-inflammatory alarmin) was independently associated with post-MI HF, outperformed traditional biomarkers, and improved risk estimation when added to traditional risk factors; Mendelian randomization supported a causal effect on HF (PMID 38538601).

Prognostic Value of Cardiovascular Biomarkers in the Population

  • Individual-participant analysis of 28 general-population cohorts (12 countries, 164,054 people, median follow-up 11.8 years) testing hs-troponin I/T, NT-proBNP, BNP and hs-CRP (PMID 38739396).
  • Every biomarker was associated with incident HF (e.g., NT-proBNP subdistribution HR 1.21 per 1-SD), and adding biomarkers to established risk factors improved discrimination and reclassification most for HF and mortality — with greater incremental value at age ≥65 (PMID 38739396).

Risk factors for the development of heart failure in patients with or without prior myocardial infarction (editorial)

  • Accompanying editorial to the HOMAGE/UK Biobank analysis; it frames the key lesson that the relative importance of HF risk factors differs by MI history and endorses targeted risk management based on MI history (PMID 35560757).
  • No abstract is available; the article is a comment on PMID 35365899 (PMID 35560757).

Risk factors and their individual headings

Age

  • Older age is the most consistently replicated predictor: per-year HR 1.03 in the STEMI cohort, and significant in both MI and non-MI groups in HOMAGE/UK Biobank (PMID 34819233; PMID 35365899).
  • Age is the most frequently included predictor across the 28 post-MI HF prediction models reviewed (PMID 40725875).

Left ventricular ejection fraction and infarct extent

  • LVEF <50% independently predicted HF (HR 1.71) and was present in 76% of patients who developed HF after STEMI (PMID 34819233).
  • Even with EF >50% and no HF at discharge, 11.8% developed HF within one year, showing that "normal" EF is not reassuring (PMID 39874177).
  • Angiographic infarct/extent surrogates — the Gensini score and multivessel disease — rank among the most common predictors in published models (PMID 40725875).

Killip class and hemodynamic compromise at presentation

  • Killip-Kimball class ≥II on admission carried an HR of 2.24 for subsequent HF in the STEMI-PCI cohort (PMID 34819233).
  • Presentational hemodynamic status is therefore a strong, immediately available discriminator at the bedside (PMID 34819233).

Diabetes mellitus

  • Diabetes independently predicted HF after STEMI (HR 1.85) (PMID 34819233), and type 2 diabetes was present in 48% of the contemporary Baylor post-MI cohort (PMID 39874177).
  • Notably, diabetes and hyperglycemia were strongly associated with new-onset HF mainly in patients without prior MI, i.e., its incremental predictive weight is attenuated once an MI has already occurred (PMID 35365899).

Renal function

  • Lower renal function was more strongly associated with HF onset in patients with prior MI than in those without (PMID 35365899), and chronic kidney disease was common (34%) in the post-MI cohort (PMID 39874177).
  • Kidney function is routinely measured and should be part of any post-MI HF risk assessment (PMID 35365899).

Reperfusion timeliness and revascularization

  • Longer door-to-balloon time independently predicted HF after primary PCI for STEMI (PMID 34819233).
  • Delays in reperfusion drive infarct size and subsequent adverse remodeling, linking treatment-process variables to HF risk (PMID 34819233).

Circulating biomarkers

  • NT-proBNP, BNP, hs-troponin I/T and hs-CRP all add independent prognostic information for incident HF, with the largest gains for HF and mortality prediction at older ages (PMID 38739396).
  • hs-CRP is among the most common model predictors (PMID 40725875), and the inflammation marker S100A8/A9 outperformed traditional biomarkers and improved risk reclassification for post-MI HF, with evidence suggesting causality (PMID 38538601).
  • Novel molecular signatures (e.g., a 13-marker immune B-cell gene model) remain exploratory but illustrate the trajectory toward biomarker-enhanced prediction (PMID 37287974).

Hypertension, BMI and metabolic burden

  • In contrast to age and renal function, hypertension, higher BMI, systolic blood pressure and blood glucose were associated with HF onset only in patients without a prior MI, with significant interaction tests (PMID 35365899).
  • This means these classic vascular risk factors matter most for primary prevention of post-MI HF, whereas post-MI risk is dominated by age, LV dysfunction, Killip class and renal function (PMID 35365899).

What this means for our team

  • The most actionable, routinely available predictors for our patients are older age, diabetes, LVEF <50%, Killip class ≥II, renal impairment, and delayed reperfusion — these are exactly the variables that anchor published post-MI HF prediction models, which achieve only moderate discrimination (C-statistic ≈0.6–0.9) and are rarely externally validated (PMID 34819233; PMID 35365899; PMID 40725875).
  • We should complement them with routinely ordered biomarkers — NT-proBNP/BNP, hs-troponin, and hs-CRP — since their incremental value is strongest for HF specifically (PMID 38739396), and watch the promising but not-yet-clinical candidates (S100A8/A9, immune gene signatures) for future implementation (PMID 38538601; PMID 37287974).
  • Given that incident HF now outnumbers recurrent MI even in normal-EF patients, a pragmatic next step is to build and internally validate our own post-MI HF risk score from variables we already collect, following TRIPOD reporting and insisting on external validation before clinical use (PMID 39874177; PMID 40725875).

2. Candidate feature sets

  • Admission set (chosen as final): age, sex, diabetes, hypertension_stage, systolic_bp_icu, time_to_hospital, prior_heart_failure, prior_mi_count, lateral_mi, anterior_mi, ecg_rhythm_sinus_tachycardia, ecg_rhythm_atrial_fibrillation, white_blood_cell_count. All are known at admission, all ≤15.5% missing, and they cover the literature's core domains (age, diabetes, hemodynamics, infarct extent, delay, rhythm).
  • Admission + severity set: the final set plus cardiogenic_shock_at_admission, pulmonary_edema_at_admission, ecg_rhythm_sinus_normal, atrial_fibrillation_at_admission, obesity. More complete hemodynamic picture, but two of these are artifact-prone or have tiny groups.
  • Through day 3 set: final set plus pain_relapse_day_1, pain_relapse_day_2, pain_relapse_day_3, opioids_icu_day_1. Useful only for prediction at later time points, not at admission.

I chose the admission set because the research question targets prediction at admission, keeps missingness low, and avoids the shock artifact and near-outcome variables.

3. Data issues

  • cardiogenic_shock_at_admission: 0.0% HF if yes (p=0.002) — severely ill patients who died early had little time to develop heart failure, so this "protective" association is an artifact; keep out of the final model.
  • systolic_bp_emergency_team and diastolic_bp_emergency_team: 62.70% missing, above our 30% threshold — excluded.
  • pain_relapse_day_1/2/3 and opioids_icu_day_1: known only after admission, so they cannot predict heart_failure at admission.
  • Tiny or non-informative groups to avoid: history_av_block_1st_degree (n=1), ecg_sinoatrial_block (n=2), history_av_block_3rd_degree (n=2), ecg_rhythm_idioventricular (no variation), obesity (n=28).
  • prior_heart_failure (90.4% HF in stage I, p<0.001) is a legitimate anamnesis predictor but is so close to the outcome that it can dominate; consider a sensitivity model without it.
  • serum_potassium / serum_sodium (~21% missing) are under 30% but not literature targets — use only if needed.

4. Predictive questions we can answer

  • Does the admission model predict in-hospital heart_failure? Outcome heart_failure; predictors the 13 final features; method multivariable logistic regression with bootstrap/cross-validated discrimination and calibration.
  • Do day-1/3 dynamics add value over the admission model? Outcome heart_failure; predictors final set plus pain_relapse_day_1 (then day 2/3); method nested logistic regression with likelihood-ratio test.
  • Is atrial fibrillation independently associated with heart_failure? Outcome heart_failure; predictors ecg_rhythm_atrial_fibrillation adjusted for the final set; method multivariable logistic regression.
  • How robust is the model without prior_heart_failure? Outcome heart_failure; predictors final set minus prior_heart_failure; method logistic regression, compare with the full model.
  • Which matters more: infarct extent (lateral_mi) or delay (time_to_hospital)? Outcome heart_failure; both predictors plus adjustment set; method logistic regression, examine coefficients/contribution.

5. Questions we cannot answer

  • Does reduced LVEF (<50%) predict heart failure? No ejection fraction, echocardiography or angiography variable exists, so the strongest literature predictor is unavailable.
  • Do NT-proBNP/BNP, hs-troponin, hs-CRP or S100A8/A9 add incremental value? None of these biomarkers are in the dataset.
  • Is renal impairment a predictor? There is no creatinine, urea or renal-function variable at all.
  • What is the effect of reperfusion timeliness (door-to-balloon) or revascularization strategy? No PCI, thrombolysis or revascularization data; time_to_hospital is only a proxy for presentation delay.

Part 3. A fixed prediction model (no language model)

The Data Analyst chose the predictors; fixed code then fitted a logistic regression and scored it. The Data Analyst did not see any of these results.

  • Patients: 1,193 train (277 with heart failure) and 507 test (117 with heart failure).
  • Predictors used (13): age, sex, diabetes, hypertension_stage, systolic_bp_icu, time_to_hospital, prior_heart_failure, prior_mi_count, lateral_mi, anterior_mi, ecg_rhythm_sinus_tachycardia, ecg_rhythm_atrial_fibrillation, white_blood_cell_count.
  • Model columns: 16 (17.3 training events per column).
  • 5-fold cross-validation in the training set: AUC 0.730 ± 0.012, PR-AUC 0.563, Brier 0.144.
  • Held-out test set: AUC 0.679, PR-AUC 0.429 (a model with no skill scores 0.231), Brier 0.159.
  • Training-set AUC (on the data the model learned from): 0.758.
